Rhetoric

My growth in rhetoric is best shown in how I edited the content I researched and the knowledge I took in based on the intended audiences and purpose for each assignment. When writing my job materials, I was careful to deliver my technical experience in a concise manner. For example, in the cover letter, I highlighted the ways in which my work experience at Amazon would help me fit into Ripple’s culture and values. For example, in the cover letter I noted how I “manag[ed] communication with almost-daily syncs with customers…and plan to use those skills to help align the technical roadmap with internal/external product partners of the Finance Primitives team.” On the other hand, analyzing the communication gap in Project Maven demanded more than just how to use rhetoric. I had to fully evaluate a rhetorical situation by identifying where the miscommunication occurred. Through this project, I was able to demonstrate how communication should not only be about clarity, but aligning the actual message with the values of the audience.

Process

The most significant example of the process would be my final project (MP3). Here my focus was no longer on improving drafts and working out technical issues, but on identifying any communication problems in the organization and suggesting effective ways to solve them. My task was to identify the problem of non-standardized onboarding for Bloomberg Terminal . Having done this, I came up with several suggestions and tools for improving the situation, including detailed workflows. I thought this showed the process in that in order to create a workflow, I had to conduct research and analyze findings to come up with solutions. Another way this project showed process was how we iterated over peer-review and teacher feedback and were able to improve our report and training materials appropriately. The main peer-review feedback we got from other groups was the overleaf of basic information, whilst not making it as actionable. This means that we had a lot of information but it was more just fun facts and would only overload beginners with information that they might not need. As such, my group and I sought to simplify our training materials by focusing on our screenshots and using those to “ground” our instruction. By focusing the breadth of information we provide to our users, we felt users would be able to maximize the skills learned.

Modes & Media

Modes and media have been the most significant part of my development in the last project that I completed which involved the creation of Notion training material that combines several modes of communication. Instead of just focusing on writing about something, I decided to incorporate visuals along with text and artificial intelligence to cater to diverse learning styles and create more accessible content. This enabled me to convey complicated procedures, such as how to use Bloomberg Terminal, in an understandable manner. In contrast to previous tasks, which mostly relied on text, this assignment showcases how I can effectively combine various modes and media to deliver my message.

Design

In terms of growth in design, my main examples would be Project Maven infographic and my Notion-based final project, which saw me evolve from a purely aesthetic approach to one centered around structure and ease of use. In the latter project, I took special care in creating clear divisions in the infographic along with shaping the correct sections in with a certain semantic relationship (i.e. putting the timeline at a top and horizontal section as it aligns with its shape). With the latter, I had to consider the way in which design choices impacted not only the visual appeal but also the readability and understandability of concepts within the format of the infographic. In my Notion-based final project, I expanded on this idea in terms of design as a whole, as my job was to develop topic-based Notion pages.
